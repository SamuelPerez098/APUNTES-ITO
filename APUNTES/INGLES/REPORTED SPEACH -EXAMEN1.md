# GET Expressions — Phrasal Verbs

> [!info] ¿Qué son las GET expressions?  
> Son expresiones formadas con **GET + partícula/preposición**. Muchas funcionan como **phrasal verbs** y cambian completamente de significado dependiendo de la partícula.
> 
> Ejemplo:
> 
> - **get** = obtener / conseguir
>     
> - **get over** = superar
>     
> - **get by** = arreglárselas
>     
> - **get around** = desplazarse / evitar / circular
>     
> - **get along** = llevarse bien
>     

---

## 1. GET OVER

**Significado:** superar, recuperarse de algo.

### Estructura

`get over + problema/enfermedad/persona/situación`

### Ejemplos

- She finally **got over** her illness.  
    → Finalmente se recuperó de su enfermedad.
    
- It took him months to **get over** the breakup.  
    → Le tomó meses superar la ruptura.
    

> [!warning] Cuidado  
> **Get over** no significa simplemente "pasar por encima".  
> En la mayoría de contextos figurativos significa **superar o recuperarse de algo**.

---

## 2. GET BY

**Significado:** arreglárselas, sobrevivir con recursos limitados.

### Estructura

`get by (on/with something)`

### Ejemplos

- I don't earn much, but I can **get by**.  
    → No gano mucho, pero puedo arreglármelas.
    
- They **get by on** a very small salary.  
    → Se las arreglan con un salario muy pequeño.
    

> [!info] Idea clave  
> **GET BY = manage to survive / manage with what you have**

---

## 3. GET AROUND

Tiene varios significados importantes.

### A) Desplazarse

- I usually **get around** by bus.  
    → Normalmente me desplazo en autobús.
    

### B) Circular / difundirse

- The news **got around** very quickly.  
    → La noticia circuló muy rápido.
    

### C) Evitar una regla o problema

- He found a way to **get around** the restrictions.  
    → Encontró una manera de evitar las restricciones.
    

> [!warning] TOEFL  
> El significado depende mucho del contexto.

---

## 4. GET ALONG

**Significado:** llevarse bien.

### Estructura

`get along (with someone)`

### Ejemplos

- Do you **get along with** your coworkers?  
    → ¿Te llevas bien con tus compañeros?
    
- They don't **get along** very well.  
    → No se llevan muy bien.
    

### Sinónimo

`get along with = get on with`

---

## 5. GET AWAY

**Significado:** escapar / alejarse / salir de vacaciones.

### A) Escapar

- The thief **got away** before the police arrived.  
    → El ladrón escapó antes de que llegara la policía.
    

### B) Irse de vacaciones

- We need to **get away** for a few days.  
    → Necesitamos salir de vacaciones unos días.
    

### GET AWAY WITH

**Significado:** hacer algo malo sin ser castigado.

- He cheated on the exam and **got away with it**.  
    → Hizo trampa en el examen y no recibió castigo.
    

> [!important] Diferencia  
> **get away** = escapar
> 
> **get away with something** = hacer algo malo y no ser castigado

---

## 6. GET BACK

**Significados principales:**

### A) Regresar

- What time did you **get back** home?  
    → ¿A qué hora regresaste a casa?
    

### B) Recuperar algo

- I need to **get back** my keys.  
    → Necesito recuperar mis llaves.
    

### C) Devolver algo

- I'll **get back to you** tomorrow.  
    → Te responderé mañana.
    

> [!tip]  
> **get back to someone** = contactar/responder a alguien posteriormente.

---

## 7. GET INTO

**Significados importantes:**

### A) Entrar

- She **got into** the car.  
    → Ella entró al automóvil.
    

### B) Empezar a interesarse en algo

- I've really **gotten into** photography.  
    → Me he interesado mucho en la fotografía.
    

### C) Meterse en problemas

- He **got into** trouble at school.  
    → Se metió en problemas en la escuela.
    

### D) Ser aceptado en una institución

- She **got into** Harvard.  
    → Fue aceptada en Harvard.
    

---

## 8. GET OUT

**Significado:** salir.

- Let's **get out** of here.  
    → Salgamos de aquí.
    

### GET OUT OF

**Significado:** evitar hacer algo / librarse de una obligación.

- He tried to **get out of** doing his homework.  
    → Intentó librarse de hacer su tarea.
    

También puede significar simplemente salir de un lugar:

- She **got out of** the car.  
    → Ella salió del automóvil.
    

---

## 9. GET ON

Tiene varios significados.

### A) Subir a transporte

- We **got on** the bus.  
    → Subimos al autobús.
    

### B) Progresar

- How are you **getting on** with your studies?  
    → ¿Cómo te va con tus estudios?
    

### C) Llevarse bien

- I **get on with** my neighbors.  
    → Me llevo bien con mis vecinos.
    

> [!warning]  
> **get on** puede significar cosas diferentes dependiendo del contexto.

---

## 10. GET OFF

### A) Bajar de un transporte

- We **got off** the train.  
    → Bajamos del tren.
    

### B) Evitar un castigo grave

- He **got off** with a warning.  
    → Se libró con una advertencia.
    

### C) Terminar el trabajo / salir del trabajo

- What time do you **get off** work?  
    → ¿A qué hora sales del trabajo?
    

---

## 11. GET UP

**Significado:** levantarse.

- I **get up** at 7:00 every morning.  
    → Me levanto a las 7:00 todas las mañanas.
    

También puede significar levantarse físicamente:

- He **got up** from the chair.  
    → Se levantó de la silla.
    

---

## 12. GET DOWN

### A) Bajar

- **Get down** from the table.  
    → Baja de la mesa.
    

### B) Deprimir / desanimar

- Don't let the situation **get you down**.  
    → No dejes que la situación te deprima.
    

### C) Concentrarse en algo

`get down to something`

- Let's **get down to work**.  
    → Pongámonos a trabajar.
    

> [!important]  
> **get down to + noun/gerund**
> 
> `get down to work`
> 
> `get down to studying`

---

## 13. GET THROUGH

Uno de los más importantes.

### A) Terminar algo difícil

- I need to **get through** this exam.  
    → Necesito superar/terminar este examen.
    

### B) Sobrevivir a una situación difícil

- She **got through** a very difficult period.  
    → Superó un período muy difícil.
    

### C) Comunicarse por teléfono

- I couldn't **get through** to the doctor.  
    → No pude comunicarme con el doctor.
    

> [!tip]  
> Piensa en **GET THROUGH = pasar al otro lado de algo**.
> 
> Puede ser:
> 
> - una dificultad
>     
> - un examen
>     
> - una etapa
>     
> - una llamada telefónica
>     

---

## 14. GET ACROSS

**Significado:** transmitir/comunicar una idea.

- The teacher couldn't **get the idea across**.  
    → El profesor no pudo transmitir la idea.
    
- She explained it clearly and **got her point across**.  
    → Lo explicó claramente y transmitió su punto de vista.
    

> [!important]  
> **get something across = make someone understand something**

---

## 15. GET AHEAD

**Significado:** progresar, tener éxito, avanzar.

- She works hard because she wants to **get ahead**.  
    → Trabaja duro porque quiere progresar.
    

`get ahead in life/career`

---

## 16. GET ALONG WITHOUT

**Significado:** arreglárselas sin algo/alguien.

- I can **get along without** a car.  
    → Puedo arreglármelas sin automóvil.
    

> Diferencia:
> 
> **get by** = arreglárselas en general
> 
> **get along without** = arreglárselas sin algo/alguien

---

## 17. GET USED TO

**Significado:** acostumbrarse a algo.

### Estructura

`get used to + noun / verb-ing`

- I'm **getting used to** the cold weather.
    
- She **got used to** working at night.
    

> [!warning] Error frecuente  
> Después de **get used to**, usamos **noun o -ing**.
> 
> ❌ I got used to work at night.
> 
> ✅ I got used to **working** at night.

# Estrategia TOEFL

> [!important] No traduzcas palabra por palabra  
> En un examen, identifica primero **qué significado necesita la oración**.

Por ejemplo:

**Although the exam was difficult, Maria managed to ______ it.**

A) get around  
B) get over  
C) get through  
D) get along

La idea es "superar/terminar un examen".

La respuesta correcta es:

**C) get through**

# Reported Speech

> [!info] ¿Qué es Reported Speech?  
> El **reported speech** (estilo indirecto) se utiliza para comunicar lo que otra persona dijo **sin repetir exactamente sus palabras**.
> 
> **Direct speech:**  
> John said, "I am tired."
> 
> **Reported speech:**  
> John said **that he was tired.**

---

# 1. Estructura básica

La estructura general para una afirmación es:

`Subject + reporting verb + (that) + reported clause`

### Ejemplo

**Direct:**

> Mary said, "I am busy."

**Reported:**

> Mary said **(that) she was busy.**

### Fórmula

```text
Subject + said + (that) + subject + verb
```

---

# 2. SAY vs TELL

Esta diferencia es MUY importante.

## SAY

`Subject + say + (that) + clause`

- He **said that** he was tired.
    
- She **said that** she needed help.
    

Normalmente **say** no lleva una persona directamente después.

❌ He said me that...

✅ He **said that**...

---

## TELL

`Subject + tell + person + (that) + clause`

- He **told me that** he was tired.
    
- She **told John that** she needed help.
    

> [!important] Regla  
> **say + something**
> 
> **tell + someone + something**

### Comparación

```text
He said that he was tired.

He told me that he was tired.
```

---

# 3. Cambios de tiempo verbal

Cuando el verbo introductorio está en pasado, normalmente hacemos **backshift**: el tiempo verbal retrocede.

|Direct Speech|Reported Speech|
|---|---|
|Present Simple|Past Simple|
|Present Continuous|Past Continuous|
|Present Perfect|Past Perfect|
|Past Simple|Past Perfect|
|Past Continuous|Past Perfect Continuous|
|will|would|
|can|could|
|may|might|

---

## Present Simple → Past Simple

Direct:

> "I work here."

Reported:

> He said that he **worked** there.

---

## Present Continuous → Past Continuous

Direct:

> "I am studying."

Reported:

> She said that she **was studying**.

---

## Present Perfect → Past Perfect

Direct:

> "I have finished the project."

Reported:

> He said that he **had finished** the project.

---

## Past Simple → Past Perfect

Direct:

> "I saw the movie."

Reported:

> She said that she **had seen** the movie.

---

## Will → Would

Direct:

> "I will call you."

Reported:

> He said that he **would call** me.

---

## Can → Could

Direct:

> "I can swim."

Reported:

> She said that she **could swim**.

---

# 4. Cambios de pronombres

Los pronombres deben cambiar dependiendo de quién está hablando.

### Direct

> John said, "I am tired."

### Reported

> John said that **he** was tired.

---

### Direct

> Mary said, "I love my parents."

### Reported

> Mary said that **she** loved **her** parents.

> [!important]  
> No cambies los pronombres automáticamente.
> 
> Pregúntate:
> 
> **¿Quién dijo la frase?**
> 
> **¿A quién se está refiriendo?**

---

# 5. Cambios de expresiones de tiempo y lugar

Algunas expresiones también pueden cambiar.

|Direct|Reported|
|---|---|
|today|that day|
|yesterday|the day before|
|tomorrow|the next day / the following day|
|now|then|
|tonight|that night|
|last week|the week before|
|next week|the following week|
|here|there|
|this|that|
|these|those|
|ago|before|

### Ejemplo

Direct:

> "I will see you tomorrow."

Reported:

> He said that he would see me **the next day**.

---

# 6. ¿Siempre tenemos que cambiar el tiempo verbal?

No.

Si la información sigue siendo verdadera o el contexto no requiere backshift, puede mantenerse el tiempo verbal.

### Ejemplo

Direct:

> The teacher said, "Water boils at 100°C."

Reported:

> The teacher said that water **boils** at 100°C.

Esto se debe a que es un **hecho general**.

> [!tip]  
> En ejercicios de examen, si no hay una razón contextual para mantener el tiempo, normalmente espera el **backshift** cuando el verbo introductorio está en pasado.

---

# 7. REPORTED SPEECH — COMMANDS

Ahora viene una estructura diferente.

Una **command** es una orden.

### Direct

> The teacher said, "Open the book."

No usamos:

❌ The teacher said that open the book.

La estructura correcta es:

`Subject + told + person + to + base verb`

### Ejemplo

Direct:

> The teacher said, "Open the book."

Reported:

> The teacher **told the students to open** the book.

---

# 8. Estructura de COMMANDS

```text
Subject + told + person + to + base verb
```

### Ejemplo

```text
She told me to wait.
```

```text
The doctor told him to take the medicine.
```

```text
The teacher told us to finish the exercise.
```

> [!important] Regla  
> Después de **told + person** usamos:
> 
> **to + infinitive**
> 
> `told me to go`
> 
> `told him to wait`
> 
> `told us to study`

---

# 9. NEGATIVE COMMANDS

Para una orden negativa:

```text
Subject + told + person + NOT TO + base verb
```

### Direct

> "Don't touch that!"

### Reported

> He **told me not to touch** that.

---

### Más ejemplos

Direct:

> "Don't open the door."

Reported:

> She told him **not to open** the door.

Direct:

> "Don't be late."

Reported:

> The teacher told us **not to be late**.

> [!warning] Error frecuente  
> ❌ He told me don't go.
> 
> ❌ He told me to don't go.
> 
> ✅ He told me **not to go**.

---

# 10. SAY vs TELL con COMMANDS

Para comandos normalmente usamos **TELL** porque necesitamos indicar a quién se dio la orden.

### Correcto

> He **told me to leave**.

### Incorrecto

> ❌ He said me to leave.

También podemos usar:

> He **said that I should leave.**

Pero para una transformación directa de una orden, la estructura más común es:

> **told + object + to + verb**

---

# 11. REPORTED SPEECH — REQUESTS

Una **request** es una petición.

Ejemplo:

> "Please help me."

La estructura típica es:

```text
Subject + asked + person + to + base verb
```

### Ejemplo

Direct:

> "Please help me."

Reported:

> She **asked me to help** her.

---

# 12. Estructura de REQUESTS

```text
Subject + asked + person + to + base verb
```

### Ejemplos

Direct:

> "Please wait here."

Reported:

> He **asked me to wait there**.

---

Direct:

> "Please close the window."

Reported:

> She **asked him to close** the window.

---

Direct:

> "Please give me some water."

Reported:

> The patient **asked the nurse to give** him some water.

---

# 13. NEGATIVE REQUESTS

Para una petición negativa:

```text
Subject + asked + person + NOT TO + base verb
```

### Direct

> "Please don't tell anyone."

### Reported

> She **asked me not to tell** anyone.

---

# 14. COMMAND vs REQUEST

La estructura gramatical es muy parecida.

|Tipo|Verbo|Estructura|
|---|---|---|
|Command|tell|told + person + to + verb|
|Request|ask|asked + person + to + verb|

### Command

> "Close the door!"

→ He **told me to close** the door.

### Request

> "Please close the door."

→ He **asked me to close** the door.

> [!important] Diferencia principal  
> **COMMAND → TELL**
> 
> **REQUEST → ASK**

---

# 15. Otros verbos útiles

No siempre tenemos que usar solamente **said, told, asked**.

## ORDER

Para órdenes fuertes:

`ordered + person + to + verb`

> The officer **ordered the soldiers to leave**.

---

## WARN

Para advertencias:

`warned + person + not to + verb`

> She **warned me not to touch** the machine.

---

## ADVISE

Para consejos:

`advised + person + to + verb`

> The doctor **advised him to rest**.

---

## REMIND

Para recordar a alguien que haga algo:

`reminded + person + to + verb`

> She **reminded me to call** my mother.

---

## INVITE

Para invitaciones:

`invited + person + to + verb`

> He **invited us to join** the party.

---

> [!important] Memoriza estas tres

### STATEMENT

```text
said + (that) + subject + verb
```

> He said **that he was tired**.

---

### COMMAND

```text
told + person + to + verb
```

> He told **me to wait**.

### NEGATIVE COMMAND

```text
told + person + not to + verb
```

> He told **me not to wait**.

---

### REQUEST

```text
asked + person + to + verb
```

> He asked **me to wait**.

### NEGATIVE REQUEST

```text
asked + person + not to + verb
```

> He asked **me not to wait**.

# 18. Truco rápido para el examen

Cuando veas una oración en reported speech, identifica primero el tipo:

```text
¿Es una afirmación?
        ↓
    said/told + clause

¿Es una orden?
        ↓
    told + person + TO

¿Es una petición?
        ↓
    asked + person + TO

¿Es negativa?
        ↓
    NOT TO
```

### Ejemplo

> "Don't touch the computer."

No es una afirmación.

Es una **negative command**.

Por lo tanto:

`TOLD + PERSON + NOT TO`

→ The teacher **told the students not to touch** the computer.

---

# 19. Errores típicos

> [!warning] Error 1 — SAY + persona

❌ He said me that he was tired.

✅ He **told me that** he was tired.

✅ He **said that** he was tired.

---

> [!warning] Error 2 — TELL sin persona

❌ He told that he was tired.

✅ He **told me that** he was tired.

---

> [!warning] Error 3 — Command con THAT

❌ She told me that open the door.

✅ She **told me to open** the door.

---

> [!warning] Error 4 — NOT mal colocado

❌ She told me to not open the door.

✅ She told me **not to open** the door.

> Aunque "to not open" puede aparecer en inglés real, para ejercicios académicos la estructura estándar que debes reconocer es **not to + verb**.

---

> [!warning] Error 5 — Confundir ASK y TELL

> "Please sit down."
> 
> → She **asked me to sit down.**

> "Sit down!"
> 
> → She **told me to sit down.**

---

# 20. Resumen final

```text
STATEMENT
said + (that) + clause

He said that he was tired.


STATEMENT CON PERSONA
told + person + (that) + clause

He told me that he was tired.


COMMAND
told + person + to + verb

He told me to wait.


NEGATIVE COMMAND
told + person + not to + verb

He told me not to wait.


REQUEST
asked + person + to + verb

He asked me to wait.


NEGATIVE REQUEST
asked + person + not to + verb

He asked me not to wait.
```

> [!success] Las 3 fórmulas esenciales  
> **STATEMENT → said + that**
> 
> **COMMAND → told + person + to**
> 
> **REQUEST → asked + person + to**
> 
> Y si es negativo:
> 
> **not to + verb**
