
# Verb Patterns

### Definition

Los **Verb Patterns** son las estructuras que determinan qué forma debe tomar el verbo que aparece después de otro verbo.

En inglés, algunos verbos deben ir seguidos de:

- **to + verb**
- **verb + -ing**
- **object + to + verb**
- **object + bare infinitive**
- **preposition + -ing**
- **that + clause**
- **object + -ing**

Esto es importante para el TOEFL porque un verbo puede requerir una estructura específica.

---

# 2. Verb + TO + Verb

### Structure

```
Subject + Verb + to + Verb (base form)
```

Algunos verbos normalmente van seguidos de **to + infinitive**.

### Common verbs

- want
- need
- hope
- plan
- decide
- expect
- agree
- promise
- refuse
- learn
- manage
- offer
- choose
- seem
- appear
- intend
- attempt
- afford

### Examples

- I **want to study**.
- She **decided to leave**.
- They **plan to travel**.
- He **refused to answer**.
- We **hope to see** you soon.

---

# 3. Verb + -ING

### Structure

```
Subject + Verb + Verb-ing
```

Algunos verbos requieren que el segundo verbo tenga la terminación **-ing**.

### Common verbs

- enjoy
- avoid
- finish
- consider
- suggest
- recommend
- mind
- keep
- practice
- admit
- deny
- imagine
- miss
- postpone
- risk

### Examples

- I **enjoy studying** English.
- She **avoids eating** sugar.
- They **finished working**.
- He **suggested going** home.
- We **considered buying** a car.

> [!warning] TOEFL  
> No todos los verbos permiten cambiar libremente `to + verb` por `verb-ing`.
> 
> ❌ I enjoy **to study** English.
> 
> ✅ I enjoy **studying** English.

---

# 4. Verb + TO + Verb / Verb + -ING

Algunos verbos pueden utilizar ambas estructuras, aunque el significado puede ser igual o diferente dependiendo del verbo.

### Common verbs

- like
- love
- hate
- prefer
- begin
- start
- continue

### Examples

- I **like to study** English.
- I **like studying** English.
- She **started to work**.
- She **started working**.

En estos casos, muchas veces ambas formas son posibles.

> [!info] Importante  
> Esto no significa que todos los verbos puedan utilizar ambas estructuras. Debes aprender cuáles permiten las dos.

# 5. Verb + Object + TO + Verb

### Structure

```
Subject + Verb + Object + to + Verb
```

Algunos verbos necesitan un objeto antes del infinitivo.

### Common verbs

- want
- ask
- tell
- advise
- allow
- encourage
- expect
- invite
- remind
- persuade
- teach
- force
- order
- need

### Examples

- I **want you to study**.
- She **asked me to help**.
- He **told us to wait**.
- They **encouraged him to apply**.
- The teacher **allowed the students to leave**.

### Compare

```
I want to study.
```

vs.

```
I want you to study.
```

En la primera:

> I → realiza la acción.

En la segunda:

> you → realiza la acción.

---

# 6. Verb + Object + Bare Infinitive

Algunos verbos utilizan un verbo en su forma base **sin `to`** después del objeto.

### Structure

```
Subject + Verb + Object + Verb (base form)
```

### Common verbs

Principalmente:

- make
- let
- have

### Examples

- She **made me laugh**.
- My parents **let me go**.
- The teacher **had us write** an essay.

> [!warning] Importante  
> Después de `make` y `let` utilizamos el verbo base sin `to`.
> 
> ✅ She made me **study**.
> 
> ❌ She made me **to study**.
> 
> ✅ They let him **leave**.
> 
> ❌ They let him **to leave**.

# 7. Help + Verb / Help + TO + Verb

`Help` es especial porque puede utilizar ambas estructuras.

### Structure

```
Subject + help + Object + Verb
```

o

```
Subject + help + Object + to + Verb
```

### Examples

- She **helped me study**.
- She **helped me to study**.

Ambas son correctas.

---

# 8. Verb + Preposition + -ING

Después de una **preposición**, si aparece un verbo, normalmente utilizamos **-ing**.

### Structure

```
Subject + Verb + Preposition + Verb-ing
```

### Examples

- She is **interested in learning** English.
- He **apologized for being** late.
- They **talked about moving**.
- I am **thinking about studying** abroad.

> [!warning] Regla importante  
> **Preposition + Verb → -ING**
> 
> ❌ interested in **learn**
> 
> ❌ interested in **to learn**
> 
> ✅ interested in **learning**

# 9. Adjective + TO + Verb

Muchos adjetivos pueden ir seguidos de **to + infinitive**.

### Structure

```
Subject + be + Adjective + to + Verb
```

### Examples

- I am **happy to help**.
- She is **ready to leave**.
- He is **afraid to speak**.
- They are **unable to attend**.
- We are **eager to learn**.

### Common adjectives

- happy
- ready
- willing
- able
- unable
- likely
- unlikely
- afraid
- surprised
- pleased
- eager
- difficult
- easy
- important

---

# 10. Adjective + Preposition + -ING

Algunos adjetivos requieren una preposición.

### Structure

```
Subject + be + Adjective + Preposition + Verb-ing
```

### Examples

- She is **interested in learning**.
- He is **good at solving** problems.
- I am **afraid of making** mistakes.
- They are **responsible for organizing** the event.

### Common combinations

|Adjective|Preposition|Example|
|---|---|---|
|interested|in|interested in learning|
|good|at|good at speaking|
|bad|at|bad at remembering|
|afraid|of|afraid of failing|
|responsible|for|responsible for managing|
|capable|of|capable of solving|
|tired|of|tired of waiting|

---

# 11. Verb + That-Clause

Algunos verbos pueden introducir una oración mediante **that**.

### Structure

```
Subject + Verb + that + Subject + Verb
```

### Common verbs

- think
- believe
- know
- say
- understand
- realize
- hope
- assume
- explain
- suggest
- claim
- agree

### Examples

- I **think that she is right**.
- They **believe that he is innocent**.
- She **said that she was tired**.
- We **know that English is important**.

`That` a menudo puede omitirse:

> I think **that** she is right.

> I think she is right.

---

# 12. Verb + Object + Adjective

Algunos verbos pueden utilizar un objeto seguido de un adjetivo.

### Structure

```
Subject + Verb + Object + Adjective
```

### Common verbs

- make
- keep
- find
- consider
- believe

### Examples

- The news **made me happy**.
- She **kept the door open**.
- I **found the exam difficult**.
- They **considered the plan impossible**.

---

# 13. Verb + Object + -ING

Algunos verbos pueden utilizar:

```
Object + Verb-ing
```

### Structure

```
Subject + Verb + Object + Verb-ing
```

### Examples

- I **saw him running**.
- She **found him sleeping**.
- We **heard them talking**.
- They **caught him stealing**.

La estructura presenta al objeto realizando la acción.

> I saw **him running**.

→ Vi **a él corriendo**.

---

# 14. Verb + Object + Past Participle

Algunos verbos pueden utilizar un objeto seguido de un **past participle**.

### Structure

```
Subject + Verb + Object + Past Participle
```

### Examples

- I **had my car repaired**.
- She **got her computer fixed**.
- We **found the door locked**.
- He **had his hair cut**.

Esta estructura es especialmente importante:

```
Have + Object + Past Participle
```

Ejemplo:

> I **had my car repaired**.

No significa necesariamente que yo reparé el automóvil.

Significa:

> Hice que repararan mi automóvil.

# 15. Gerund vs Infinitive

Una de las partes más importantes de **Verb Patterns** es saber cuándo utilizar:

```
Verb + to + Verb
```

o

```

```

### Verb + -ING

- enjoy
- avoid

- suggest

# 16. Verbos que cambian de significado

Algunos verbos cambian de significado dependiendo de si utilizamos **to + verb** o **-ing**.

Los más importantes son:

### Remember

```
Remember + to + Verb
```

→ recordar hacer algo.

> Remember **to call** me.

```
Remember + Verb-ing
```

→ recordar algo que ocurrió.

> I remember **calling** you.

---

### Forget

```
Forget + to + Verb
```

→ olvidar hacer algo.

> I forgot **to call** him.

```
Forget + Verb-ing
```

→ olvidar una experiencia o acción pasada.

> I'll never forget **meeting** her.

---

### Stop

```
Stop + to + Verb
```

→ detener una acción para hacer otra.

> He stopped **to smoke**.

```
Stop + Verb-ing
```

→ dejar de hacer algo.

> He stopped **smoking**.

---

### Try

```
Try + to + Verb
```

→ intentar hacer algo.

> Try **to open** the door.

```
Try + Verb-ing
```

→ probar un método para solucionar algo.

> Try **opening** the window.

---

### Regret

```
Regret + to + Verb
```

→ lamentar tener que comunicar algo.

> We regret **to inform** you that your application was rejected.

```
Regret + Verb-ing
```

→ lamentar algo que hicimos.

> I regret **saying** that.

---
